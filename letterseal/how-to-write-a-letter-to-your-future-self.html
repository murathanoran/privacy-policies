How to write a letter to your future self

A letter to your future self is a message you seal today and open on a chosen day, months or years from now. It costs nothing but half an hour, and on the day it opens it is one of the most moving things you will ever read, because the writer knew you better than anyone.

1. Decide when it opens

The date shapes the letter. A letter that opens in a month is a check-in; one that opens in ten years is a time capsule. Good choices:

  • New Year's Day. Written in December, opened a year or more later: the classic. Resolutions are easier to keep when someone is going to ask.
  • Your next birthday. A yearly ritual that builds into a shelf of letters.
  • The end of something. Graduation, the first year in a new city or job, the day a baby turns one.
  • A surprise. Let the date be chosen for you, so the letter finds you on an ordinary day.

2. Start with an ordinary day

The details you think are too boring to write down are the ones you will lose first: what you had for breakfast, the song you keep replaying, the commute, the people you text most. Describe where you are sitting as you write. In a year these lines will matter more than the big news.

3. Be honest about hopes and worries

Write what you are hoping for and what you are afraid of, specifically. "I hope the move works out" says less than "I'm scared I'll be lonely in a city where I know two people." Your future self will know how it turned out; give them something real to compare against.

4. Ask questions only they can answer

Questions turn the letter into a conversation across time. Did you do the thing you keep saying you'll do? Are you still friends with the people who matter today? Was the worry worth it?

5. Be kind

Plans change. Write as if to a friend who may have had a hard year: proud of what they tried, not keeping score. One line that helps on a bad day is worth more than a list of goals.

6. Seal it, then leave it alone

A letter you can reread whenever you like loses its power. Seal it somewhere it stays closed until the date. If you write in an app, check that it keeps the letter on your device, does not need an account that might disappear, and will still open the letter if you change phones.

30 questions to start from

Right now

  • What does an ordinary day look like for you right now?
  • What's taking up most of your thoughts this week?
  • Who are the people you spend the most time with?
  • What song, show or book is part of your life at the moment?
  • How are you, honestly?

Hopes

  • What are you hoping will be different by the time you read this?
  • What are you working toward, and why does it matter to you?
  • Where do you hope you'll be living when this opens?
  • What habit do you hope you've kept?
  • What kind of person are you trying to become?

Worries

  • What feels uncertain, and how do you hope it turns out?
  • What decision are you struggling with?
  • What's hard at the moment that nobody else can see?
  • If things haven't gone the way you hoped, what do you want to say to yourself?

Advice

  • What do you know now that you don't want to unlearn?
  • What should you stop worrying about?
  • What's a mistake you don't want to repeat?
  • What would you tell yourself on a bad day?

Questions for the reader

  • Did you do the thing you keep saying you'll do?
  • Do you still want what you want right now?
  • What changed that you didn't see coming?
  • Are you kinder to yourself than you are today?
  • Was the thing you were worried about worth the worry?

For New Year

  • What's one promise to yourself you'd like to check on a year from now?
  • What did this year teach you that you want to keep?
  • What are you leaving behind with this year?
  • What do you want next year to feel like, more than look like?
  • If next New Year's Day goes well, what will have happened?

For someone else

  • What do you hope for them by the time they read this?
  • What would you tell them on a hard day?
