RULE

Logic puzzles with one answer

A pattern puzzle is a small promise: look closely and you will find the one thing that is true. When two answers can be defended, the promise breaks — and the player learns to guess rather than reason.

1. Why pattern puzzles go wrong

Any finite set of examples fits more than one rule. A circle that becomes a square, and a square that becomes a … what? Perhaps "every shape gains a corner", perhaps "circles become squares and nothing else changes", perhaps "the second shape is always a square". A designer who only checks the rule they had in mind will miss the others, and the player who found a different one is told they are wrong.

2. Write the rule down as data

In RULE, a rule is not a sentence in someone's head. It is data the game can run: "turn every shape a quarter turn clockwise", "fill only the last shape", "put each shape inside a circle". Because the rule can be run, the game can check that it really produces every example shown and the correct answer — and that no wrong option is also produced by it.

3. Look for the rival rules

The harder part is the rules the designer did not intend. RULE keeps a catalogue of 556 rival rules — shape changes, fills, sizes, rotations, mirrors, counts, positions, containment, and combinations of them. For each puzzle, every rival is run against the same examples. If a rival explains all of them and points at a different option, the puzzle is ambiguous, and it is redesigned before it ships.

4. Difficulty is not ambiguity

A hard puzzle asks you to notice something subtle: a change that happens only to the last shape, or a shape that stays still while everything else moves. That is fair. An ambiguous puzzle asks you to read the designer's mind. That is not. Checking every puzzle against its rivals lets the hard ones be as hard as they like and still be fair.

5. What a catalogue cannot do

556 rival rules are many, but not all the rules a person could imagine. That is why every authored puzzle in RULE was also read and approved by a person before it shipped, and why a player who finds a second reading is asked to say so on the support page: a puzzle that turns out to have one is changed.

6. The same checks for puzzles made on your device

The Daily Rule and Endless are generated on your iPhone or iPad, so no server is needed. Each generated puzzle goes through the same checks — the rule produces every example, no wrong option is produced by it, and no rival rule points elsewhere — before it is shown. One that fails is discarded.

RULE is a calm deduction game for iPhone and iPad: 270 handmade puzzles in ten worlds, a free Daily Rule every day, free hints, no timer, no ads, no account.
