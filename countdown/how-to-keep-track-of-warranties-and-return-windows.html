How to keep track of warranties and return windows

Most people lose money on returns and warranties not because they don't have the right, but because the date passed quietly. A small routine fixes that.

1. Two dates come with almost every purchase

The return window is the store's promise: bring it back by a certain day and get your money back. It is usually short — often 14 to 90 days — and every store sets its own.

The warranty covers defects for longer, often one or two years from the purchase date. Some products come with a manufacturer warranty on top of what the law gives you; in the European Union, for example, consumers have a legal guarantee of at least two years against the seller. The rules differ by country, so check the ones that apply to you.

2. Write the dates down the day you buy

The receipt has everything: purchase date, store, price and often the return policy. Note the last day to return and the warranty end date right away, while the receipt is in your hand. Later you will not remember which store gave 30 days and which gave 14.

3. Holiday purchases need extra care

Many stores extend return windows for purchases made in November and December, often into January — but the last day differs from store to store, and sometimes by product. Look for it on the receipt or the store's website, and write down the exact day for each gift you buy.

4. Get reminded before, not on, the last day

A reminder on the last day is often too late: you still have to find the box, print a label or get to the store. A week before gives you room; two days before is the minimum for most returns.

5. Keep the proof with the date

For a warranty claim you usually need proof of purchase, the purchase date and sometimes the serial number. Keep a photo of the receipt with the item's dates, and note the serial number when you unpack it — it is easier than turning the appliance over later.

6. Archive, don't delete

When a return window closes, the warranty may still be running. Keep the record until the warranty ends, then archive it — you may need the purchase date again for insurance or resale.
