100

Privacy Policy

Effective Date: October 17, 2026

Last Updated: October 17, 2026 (version 3.0)

Introduction

100 helps you do one thing a hundred times and keep a record of each one. You choose an activity, and every time you do it you fill one of a hundred units on a grid, optionally with a short note or a photo. There are no streaks, no deadlines, and nothing that resets or expires. The app works without accounts, tracking, analytics, advertising, or any developer-side storage of what you create. Everything you record stays on your own devices — your iPhone and, if you use it, your Apple Watch.

Information We Do Not Collect

The developer does not collect any personal information. Specifically, the developer does not collect:

  • Name, email address, phone number, or physical address
  • Contact list or address book data
  • Your photos, notes, or anything else you record in the app
  • Precise or approximate location data
  • Financial account information or payment card information
  • Advertising identifiers or device identifiers used for tracking
  • Analytics, usage, crash, or behavioral data

Information Stored Only on Your Devices

Everything you create is written to the app's private storage on your iPhone:

  • your hundreds: the name and symbol you choose, the dates they were started and finished, your optional reminder settings, and whether one is a second or later round;
  • each repetition you record: its date and time, an optional note you write, and an optional photo you add;
  • whether you have seen the welcome screen, whether the app has already shown its one-time request to rate it, and whether 100 Premium is unlocked on this device.

Photos. If you add a photo to a repetition (100 Premium), the app saves its own copy, reduced in size and re-encoded with all location, camera and time metadata removed. Choosing a photo from your library uses Apple's photo picker, which gives 100 only the photo you pick and no access to the rest of your library. Taking a photo uses the camera only when you choose "Take Photo". Photos are never uploaded.

Widgets, Control Center and Siri. To show your progress in widgets and controls, and to record a repetition from a widget, a Control Center control, the Lock Screen, the Action button or Siri and Shortcuts, the app keeps a small summary (the name, symbol and current count of the hundreds you have in progress) and any repetitions waiting to be added, in a private container shared only between 100 and its own extensions on the same device.

Apple Watch. If you use 100 on Apple Watch, your iPhone sends that same small summary to your paired watch, and repetitions you record on the watch are sent back to your iPhone. This happens directly between your own iPhone and your own Apple Watch through Apple's WatchConnectivity framework; it never passes through the developer or any server operated by the developer. The watch keeps the summary, and any repetitions your iPhone has not yet received, in its own private storage.

Backup files. If you choose "Back up everything", the app creates a single file containing your hundreds, entries, notes and photos, and hands it to you through the iOS share sheet. You decide where it goes — for example the Files app, iCloud Drive, AirDrop or a computer — and that destination's own terms apply. 100 never uploads a backup anywhere by itself. Restoring reads only a backup file you choose, and only adds what is missing on this iPhone.

Device backups. Your data is included in your iPhone's own iCloud or computer backup, under your control, like any other app's data. 100 does not use iCloud sync, and none of this data is transmitted to the developer or any external server.

App Permissions

100 asks for at most two permissions, each only at the moment it is needed and never when the app is first opened:

  • Notifications — only if you switch on a reminder for one of your hundreds. Reminders are local notifications created and delivered by your own device.
  • Camera — only when you choose "Take Photo".

Declining either changes nothing else: every other part of the app keeps working, and you can still add photos from your library. 100 never asks for location, contacts, microphone, health data, or tracking permission.

Purchases

100 Premium is a one-time in-app purchase, not a subscription. It is processed entirely by Apple through the App Store. The developer never receives your payment details or your Apple Account information; the app only learns from Apple whether Premium has been purchased on your Apple Account, so that it can unlock it and restore it on your other devices. If you bought 100 before it became free, the app checks the App Store receipt on your device to recognise that, so you never pay twice. Apart from your own paired Apple Watch, this is the only time the app communicates with anything outside your iPhone, and it is with Apple.

Third-Party Services

This app does not integrate third-party analytics services, advertising networks, or tracking SDKs, and it contains no third-party code or libraries. Apart from Apple's own App Store services for the in-app purchase, everything the app does runs on your device.

Data Sharing

The developer does not sell, trade, rent, or share user data with third parties, and there is no developer-side database of user-created content. Sharing happens only when you choose it — for example when you share a progress picture, a mosaic image, a CSV export or a backup file through the iOS share sheet. What happens to it then is up to you and the app you share it with.

Data Deletion

You can delete a single repetition, a note, a photo, or a whole hundred from within the app; deleting a hundred also deletes every repetition, note and photo recorded for it. Deleting the app removes all of its data from your iPhone, including the summary used by widgets and controls; deleting 100 from your Apple Watch removes the watch's copy of the summary. The developer holds no copy of anything. The only copies outside the app are backup files or exports you chose to create, which stay wherever you saved them until you delete them.

Children's Privacy

This app does not knowingly collect personal information from children or from any users, as it does not collect personal information in general.
