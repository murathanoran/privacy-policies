How much can I spend today?

Most budgets look back: where did the money go last month? A daily budget looks forward, one day at a time. It takes three numbers and a minute.

1. Start with what you really have

Take the money you have until your next payday — or for the month, or for a trip. If you are paid on a schedule, start from your take-home pay and set aside first what has to be paid before then: rent, bills, and anything you want to save.

2. Divide by the days it has to last

What is left, divided by the days until payday, is today's share. 600 over 30 days is 20 a day. That one number is easier to keep in your head than a spreadsheet of categories.

3. Let each day move the next

Spend 12 today and the 8 you didn't spend is spread over the days ahead; spend 30 and the coming days get a little less. Either way, the total still adds up: on the last day you can spend exactly what is left. Some people prefer a fixed envelope instead — the same amount every day, with savings piling up — and that works too.

4. Ask before the big purchase

Before buying something larger, check what it does to the days that remain. If it leaves them thin, you'll know before you pay, not after.

5. Log as you go — it's quicker than you think

The method only works if spending is written down. Make it a three-second habit: a favourite for the coffee you buy every day, a widget on the Lock Screen, or saying it to Siri. A short evening check-in catches what you forgot.

Why not just link the bank?

Bank-linked apps see every transaction, but they see it late, they need your credentials, and they send your financial life to a server. Typing what you spend takes seconds, and keeps it on your phone. It also makes you notice each purchase — which is half the point.
